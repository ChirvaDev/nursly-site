**A 4 on 4 off schedule means you work four 12-hour shifts in a row, then have four days off, over and over.** The cycle is 8 days long, so your work days move one weekday later every week. You average 42 hours a week and have half the days of the year off.

## How the 4 on 4 off pattern works

The rule is simple: 4 shifts, 4 days off, repeat. What surprises most people is what that does to the week. Because the cycle is 8 days and a week is 7, **every week your shifts start one day later**. After 8 weeks the pattern lines up with the same weekdays again.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/blog/4-on-4-off-schedule/cycle" w=1280 h=980 alt="8 weeks of a 4 on 4 off schedule starting on a Monday: the block of four shifts moves one day later each week and lines up again after 8 weeks" caption="Eight weeks of 4 on 4 off, starting on a Monday. Yellow = 12-hour shift. The block moves one day later every week." %}

So you won't have the same days off every week. One week you're off Friday to Monday, a few weeks later you work the whole weekend. Writing it down — or putting it in a calendar app — matters more on 4 on 4 off than on most schedules.

4 on 4 off is common in police, fire and EMS, manufacturing and security. Some hospital units use it too, especially for 12-hour teams that cover 24/7, but most staff nurses work self-scheduled three 12s instead.

## How four teams cover 24/7

To staff every day and every night, a unit needs four teams. Two teams share the day shift — while one works its 4 days, the other has its 4 days off — and two teams share the nights the same way.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/blog/4-on-4-off-schedule/teams" w=1280 h=560 alt="Four teams on 4 on 4 off over 16 days: teams A and B alternate day shifts, teams C and D alternate night shifts" caption="Teams A and B swap the days, teams C and D swap the nights." %}

There's also a **rotating version**: 4 days, 4 off, 4 nights, 4 off — a 16-day cycle, so you work days and nights equally. ## 4 on 4 off and overtime

Here 4 on 4 off is easier to plan around than most 12-hour schedules. Because the block of four shifts slides through the week, your pay weeks follow the same rhythm every time: **four weeks of 48 hours, then four weeks of 36 hours** — no matter which day you start.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/blog/4-on-4-off-schedule/hours" w=1280 h=660 alt="Eight pay weeks on a 4 on 4 off schedule: four weeks of 48 hours with 8 overtime hours each, then four weeks of 36 hours" caption="Hours per 7-day pay week over one 8-week cycle. The darker part is overtime." %}

That's 8 hours of overtime in each 48-hour week: **32 overtime hours every 8 weeks, or about 210 a year**. Only the order changes depending on your start day — you might begin with the 36-hour weeks instead.

This assumes overtime after 40 hours in a 7-day pay week, as is usual in the US, and 12 paid hours per shift. Over 14-day periods, 4 on 4 off ranges from 72 to 96 hours, so hospitals that use a 14-day overtime period (8/80) or union contracts with their own rules can count it differently.

**Example, before differentials:** at $40 an hour, a 48-hour week pays 40 × $40 = $1,600 plus 8 × $60 = $480 — **$2,080**. A 36-hour week pays **$1,440**. ## Pros and cons of 4 on 4 off

**Pros**

- Four days off every time — a mini-vacation every 8 days.
- Simple to remember: there's only one rule.
- Half the days of the year off.
- Overtime is predictable: four heavier weeks, then four lighter ones.
- On the fixed version you stay on days or on nights, so your body clock doesn't keep switching.

**Cons**

- Four 12-hour shifts in a row is 48 hours of work in 4 days — the last shift is tough, especially on nights.
- Your days move every week, so recurring commitments (classes, childcare, a weekly team sport) are hard to keep.
- You work about half of all weekends.
- The first day off often goes to recovering, so it can feel more like 3 days off than 4.

## Getting through four 12s in a row

- **Prep before the block:** meals for four days, clothes, a full tank of gas. You won't want to do any of it on day 3.
- **Keep the in-between hours simple:** eat, shower, sleep. Errands can wait for the days off.
- **Protect your sleep:** 7+ hours between shifts if you can. On nights, blackout curtains and a fixed sleep time help.
- **Plan day 1 off as a recovery day,** and put appointments and plans on days 2–4.

## Common mistakes with 4 on 4 off

- **Assuming your days off repeat weekly.** They don't — check the calendar before you agree to anything recurring.
- **Planning the budget around one week.** Your paychecks alternate between heavier and lighter stretches of four weeks.
- **Booking plans on the first day off.** After four 12s you'll probably want to sleep.
- **Losing count mid-block.** "Is this shift 3 or 4?" happens more than you'd think — a calendar with the whole rotation fixes it.

## Questions

**How many hours a week is 4 on 4 off?**
42 hours on average. In real pay weeks you work 48 hours four weeks in a row, then 36 hours for four weeks.

**Is 4 on 4 off full time?**
Yes. About 2,190 hours a year is more than a standard 40-hour full-time job (2,080 hours), and it usually includes around 210 hours of overtime.

**How many weekends off do you get on 4 on 4 off?**
3 full weekends off every 8 weeks, about 20 a year. You work roughly half of all weekend days.

**Do you work the same days every week on 4 on 4 off?**
No. The cycle is 8 days long, so your shifts move one weekday later each week. The pattern lines up with the same weekdays again every 8 weeks.

**Is 4 on 4 off better than Pitman?**
It depends on what you value. 4 on 4 off gives longer breaks (4 days) but longer stretches of work (4 shifts). Pitman never has more than 3 shifts in a row and gives every other weekend off, but its breaks are only 2–3 days.

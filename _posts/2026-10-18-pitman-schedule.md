**The Pitman schedule is a 14-day rotation of 12-hour shifts: 2 days on, 2 off, 3 on, 2 off, 2 on, 3 off.** It's also called the **2-2-3 schedule**. You never work more than three shifts in a row, you get every other weekend off, and you average 42 hours a week.

## The Pitman pattern

{% include figure.html src="/assets/blog/pitman-schedule/cycle" w=1280 h=668 alt="Pitman 14-day cycle starting Monday. Week 1: work Monday, Tuesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Week 2: work Wednesday and Thursday" caption="One Pitman cycle starting on a Monday. Yellow = 12-hour shift." %}

Then it repeats. Over two weeks you work **7 shifts** and have **7 days off**. In week 1 you work Friday to Sunday; in week 2 you have a 3-day weekend off.

## How hospitals use it for 24/7 coverage

A unit that needs nurses around the clock usually runs Pitman with **four teams**: two teams work days and two work nights, each pair on opposite halves of the cycle. That way someone always covers each 12-hour shift, every day of the year.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/blog/pitman-schedule/teams" w=1280 h=552 alt="Four teams on Pitman: teams A and B alternate day shifts, teams C and D alternate night shifts" caption="Four teams cover every day and every night of the year." %}

Many units keep each team on fixed days or fixed nights. Others rotate between days and nights every two or four weeks — a variation sometimes called the 2-2-3 rotating schedule. You can preview both in the [generator]({{ '/12-hour-shift-schedule?p=rot223' | relative_url }}).

## Pitman and overtime

A 42-hour average doesn't mean 42 hours every week. In the US, overtime is usually counted per 7-day pay week, and with Pitman most pay weeks alternate between **36 hours and 48 hours**. For example, with a Sunday-to-Saturday pay week and the cycle starting on a Monday, you work 3 shifts one week and 4 the next.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/blog/pitman-schedule/hours" w=1280 h=660 alt="Six Pitman pay weeks alternating 36 and 48 hours, with 8 overtime hours above the 40-hour line every other week" caption="Pay weeks alternate between 36 and 48 hours. The darker part is overtime." %}

The 48-hour weeks usually mean **8 hours of overtime every other week** — around 208 overtime hours a year. That's why hospitals don't always use Pitman for staff nurses, and why some contracts handle it differently: some use a 14-day overtime period (Pitman has 84 hours in 14 days, so 4 hours over 80), and union contracts may have their own rules.

## Pros and cons of the Pitman schedule

**Pros**

- Every other weekend off, as a 3-day weekend.
- Never more than 3 shifts in a row.
- Half the days of the year off.
- A predictable pattern — you can plan months ahead.
- The 48-hour weeks often bring overtime pay.

**Cons**

- 12-hour shifts are tiring, and three in a row can be hard, especially on nights.
- Weeks alternate between light and heavy, which can make childcare and appointments tricky.
- Days off are spread out: you only get 2–3 days off in a row, never a long break like [DuPont's]({{ '/guides/dupont-schedule/' | relative_url }}) 7 days.
- On rotating variations, switching between days and nights disrupts sleep.
